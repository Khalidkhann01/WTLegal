Privacy Policy for WhosThere

Last updated: 24 November 2026

WhosThere ("the app") is an Android app that protects your phone and your private things. It takes a photo of whoever tries to get into your phone, keeps a private vault for your photos, videos, files and notes, can lock other apps, and can disguise itself on your home screen. This policy explains what the app accesses, what it stores, and what it shares.

The short version: WhosThere has no servers, no accounts, no ads and no analytics. Everything stays on your phone. The released app does not even have permission to use the internet, and it does not take part in Android cloud backup. We do not collect, sell or share your data.

1. Data the app handles

All of the following is created on your device and stored only on your device, in the app's private storage and a local database:

DataWhy it is used
Intruder photos (front camera)Taken when a trigger you have switched on fires: wrong unlock attempts, a SIM card change, the charger being unplugged, airplane mode or power-off, or the screen turning on. They show you who tried to get in.
Event detailsThe time and type of each trigger, shown next to the photo.
Screenshots of other appsOnly if you turn on screenshot monitoring: pictures of the screen while another app is open, to show what was done. You choose which apps to skip.
Activity logWhich apps were opened and for how long while protection was on, read from Android's usage statistics. Only if you allow usage access.
List of installed appsRead on your phone to let you pick apps to lock or to skip. It is not stored outside the app and never leaves the phone.
Vault filesPhotos, videos and files you choose to add. They are copied into the app's private storage. There is a real vault and an optional decoy vault.
Secure notesThe text, formatting, pictures, colours and decorations of notes you write. Protected notes ask for your code again before they open.
Locked apps listWhich apps you chose to lock, so the app can cover them until you unlock.
PINs, patterns, passwords and codesYour app PIN, vault code and decoy code. They are kept in the phone's secure storage and are not shared with anyone.
App settingsYour choices, such as triggers, lock delay, theme, colours, app icon disguise and the tour you have seen.

2. Permissions, and when the app asks

WhosThere asks for a permission only when you use the feature that needs it. Nothing is asked when you first open the app.

  • Camera, Device administrator, Notifications, Battery optimisation exemption: asked when you turn protection on, and only the ones still missing. The camera takes the intruder photo. Device administrator lets the app notice failed unlock attempts and makes uninstalling it harder for an intruder; it does not let us lock, wipe or control your phone. Notifications tell you when something was caught. The battery exemption and the foreground service keep protection running while the phone is locked and after a restart.
  • Usage access: asked on the Activity tab. It lets the app show which apps were used.
  • Accessibility service: asked when you turn on screenshots of other apps, after a clear explanation. It is used only to notice which app is on screen so a screenshot can be taken. It does not read, record or send what you type or what is on your screen, other than the screenshots you enabled, which stay on your phone.
  • Notification access: asked when you lock apps, so that notifications from locked apps can be hidden. The app does not store or send those notifications.
  • Photos and media: asked when you add items to the vault or restore them. When you add items, the app asks Android to remove the originals from your gallery, and Android shows its own confirmation.
  • Phone state: detect a SIM change.
  • Biometrics (fingerprint): optional. Android checks your fingerprint and only tells the app yes or no. The app never receives your fingerprint.
  • Run at startup: bring protection back after a restart.

You can take back any permission in Android settings at any time. The matching feature then stops working.

3. App disguise and privacy features

  • Disguise: you can make the app look like a Calculator, Clock or Flashlight on your home screen. It opens with your app PIN. The flashlight uses the phone's torch and takes no photos.
  • Hidden previews: the app hides its screen in the recent-apps list and covers it when you leave, so private content is not shown.
  • Decoy vault: a second code opens a clean-looking vault, in case someone forces you to open it.

4. What we do not do

  • We do not run servers that receive your data, and the released app has no internet permission.
  • We do not upload your photos, screenshots, vault, notes or settings anywhere. There is no cloud backup.
  • We do not collect, sell, rent or share personal data.
  • We do not use advertising, analytics or tracking tools.
  • We do not require an account.

If you choose to share or export something yourself (for example a photo from Evidence or a vault file), it goes where you send it, through Android's own share screen. That is your choice and outside the app.

5. Data retention and deletion

Your data stays on your device until you delete it. You can delete single captures, vault items and notes in the app, and the app can remove old captures automatically using limits you set under Saved photos. Erase everything in Settings (it asks for your PIN) removes all data and settings and returns the app to a brand new state. Uninstalling the app also removes everything it stored.

6. Security

Your codes are kept in the phone's secure storage. The app is protected by your PIN, pattern or password, and an optional fingerprint. Turning protection off or changing sensitive settings asks for your PIN again. Wrong codes make the app wait before you can try again. No method of storage is perfectly secure, and keeping your phone's own screen lock on is part of keeping your data safe.

7. Children

WhosThere is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them.
